Mont Blanc gear: what worked and what was too much

Gear for Mont Blanc easily gets out of hand. This is what I took, what worked and what I would change.

Marcin Wnuk, normal route via the Goûter, June 2026

In short

  • My rucksack weighed about 20.2 kg without water. Too much.
  • I lightened it in stages: in the car, at Tête Rousse and at the Goûter.
  • Best buys: a jacket whose hood fits over a helmet, mittens, and goggles as a backup.
  • Biggest mistake: approach shoes from Bellevue to Tête Rousse.

The rucksack and cutting weight

I packed several times, weighed, rearranged and took things out, and still had too much. Some of the weight came from going independently, acclimatising and carrying gear for different scenarios. Some of it was simply excess.

After Gran Paradiso I left some freeze-dried meals, spare bars, raisins, microspikes and approach shoes in the car. More stayed at Tête Rousse, and before the summit I left spare items, dirty clothes and my wash bag at the Goûter. I wore some of the clothes instead of carrying them. Before Tête Rousse and the Goûter every kilogram counts.

Owning the gear is not enough. You need to know what is to hand, what is deeper in the pack, what you put on before leaving, what you add at the Vallot and what stays at the hut.

What worked

  • layers: base layer, active layer, softshell, and insulation and a hardshell when needed,
  • a Primaloft jacket bought for Mont Blanc, with a hood that fits over a helmet,
  • a hooded Polartec Alpha top, warm but less bulky than thick fleece,
  • mittens as a warmth reserve when it got really cold,
  • automatic crampons, quick to put on even in gloves.

What I would change

  • from Bellevue to Tête Rousse I would wear mountaineering boots from the start, because carried in the pack they took almost a third of the space,
  • I would take a second pair of glasses: mine broke inside their case and my goggles saved me,
  • less food, fewer spares and less electronics,
  • I would test the crampons with the actual boots at home,
  • covers for the ice axe and crampons are a must, in the van, the pack and the hut.

Next time I would sort everything into essential, very useful, emergency, comfort and unnecessary. On a first trip like this you fear running short, so you make the opposite mistake and carry too much.
