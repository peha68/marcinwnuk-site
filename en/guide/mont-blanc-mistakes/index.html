The biggest mistakes on Mont Blanc and a plan B

Even a good trip has mistakes. What matters is having the margin to fix them. This is my list, and the plan B worth having before you go.

Marcin Wnuk, normal route via the Goûter, June 2026

Eleven mistakes

Few of them were dramatic. Instead of avalanches or storms it was more often a rucksack that was too heavy, too much food or an email not read carefully.

  1. A rucksack that was too heavy. About 20.2 kg without water. Spare kit is necessary, but you should be able to justify every kilogram.
  2. Too much food. With nights in huts, many of the freeze-dried meals were unnecessary. On the route bars and raisins were enough.
  3. Too much electronics. Several power banks “just in case” add up. Mobile data went on sending videos and, most likely, automatic photo syncing. Turn it off and keep maps and documents offline.
  4. Too little lip protection. Sun, wind and snow work fast. Lip balm with SPF belongs in your pocket.
  5. Too much faith in the app. Between Bellevue and Tête Rousse the map didn’t always lead the obvious way. A GPS track helps, but your eyes and judgement matter more.
  6. A mistake with the vehicle. An email not checked carefully and an unclear pick-up point. I now confirm a hire vehicle as carefully as a hut: place, time, documents, deposit, drivers and permission to cross borders.
  7. No rest plan after the descent. I drove most of the way home. A trip ends when you are safely home, not when you step off the gondola.
  8. Too little casual footage. What shows a trip best is what happens between the views: packing, the chaos in the hut, tiredness, conversations.
  9. Testing some gear too late. Rope attachment, gloves, access to food, packing for the summit. Critical things are tested at home, not at 3 am in the Goûter.
  10. Summit pressure. Cost, holidays and bookings build pressure. Give yourself permission to turn back while still at home.
  11. Going without seeing a cardiologist. If you have had serious health problems, see a doctor before the trip. Better to learn the risk in a consulting room than at altitude.

Plan B

If you pay for huts, transport and insurance and take time off work, you naturally want to reach the top. But the mountains have no duty to let us up. Before the trip it is worth answering a few questions:

  • What do we do if the weather doesn’t allow a start?
  • Do we have a spare day, and can we move the plan?
  • Is there another, easier objective nearby?
  • Do we wait in Chamonix or go home early?
  • Does the group see no summit as a failure or as part of the risk?

The worst plan B is none at all. Then, in bad weather, the thinking starts: “since we’re here anyway, let’s give it a go”. That is how most bad decisions in the mountains begin.

What I would do the same

  • finding the team early and doing a trial trip together,
  • Gran Paradiso for acclimatisation,
  • two nights at the Goûter,
  • the van as a logistics base,
  • documents offline and weather checked in several sources,
  • the rule: safety before the summit.
