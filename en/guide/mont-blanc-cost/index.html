How much does Mont Blanc cost without a guide

An independent trip to Mont Blanc is much cheaper than a commercial one, but it is not cheap. This is how our budget per person worked out in June 2026.

Marcin Wnuk, normal route via the Goûter, June 2026

In short

  • Huts (Tête Rousse, two nights at the Goûter, Vittorio): about €496 per person.
  • Shared transport split between five people: van, fuel, vignettes, tolls, tunnels, parking.
  • Insurance on top, and not the place to save money.
  • Gear counted separately, because everyone starts with different kit.

Decide what you are counting

One person counts only the car, fuel and huts. Another adds every energy bar. A third puts new boots, a jacket, a watch and a rucksack into the budget. Then everyone says “Mont Blanc cost me this much”, and the numbers can’t be compared. That is why I split costs into three levels:

LevelWhat it covers
Cost of the triptransport, fuel, vignettes, accommodation, huts, water, food, insurance
Essential gearboots, crampons, ice axe, helmet, harness, clothing, rope, glasses
Long-term gearwatch, camera, extra power banks, a better rucksack

Only the first level is fair to compare with a commercial trip.

Huts: the biggest fixed cost

For our trip the high mountain huts came to about €496 per person: Tête Rousse, two nights at the Goûter and Rifugio Vittorio Emanuele II. With huts it is easy to count something twice. You pay a deposit in advance, then get a receipt at the hut for the balance and food, and water and drinks come on top. The full breakdown is in the hut booking guide.

Transport is shared

Our shared costs were mostly the van, fuel, vignettes, road tolls, tunnels, parking and some of the accommodation. With five people this splits sensibly. The same van for three people would cost much more each. With six it would be cheaper, but the amount of gear and the logistics grow. How much transport costs you depends on where you start, so I don’t quote our figure here.

Insurance

We budgeted for Alpenverein membership or a separate policy. I would not save money here. On a high mountain trip insurance is about rescue and transport, and a European Health Insurance Card is not enough for that. Check the terms, premiums and cover before your own trip, because they change.

Gear is a separate account

For one person the biggest cost is the trip and the huts. For another it is boots, crampons, an ice axe, a jacket, a rucksack and glasses. Things you will use for years should not be added to the cost of one trip without comment.
