Mont Blanc via the Goûter route: day by day

Six days in the mountains, from Pont in the Aosta Valley to Bellevue above Les Houches. Times come from my watch, distances and height gain from cleaned GPS tracks.

Marcin Wnuk, normal route via the Goûter, June 2026

In short

  • Six days in the mountains: 41.3 km and about 5,080 m of ascent.
  • Acclimatisation on Gran Paradiso (4,061 m) on 10 June.
  • Start from Bellevue, because the Tramway du Mont-Blanc was not running to Nid d’Aigle.
  • Summit on 13 June, with two nights at the Goûter.

9 June: Pont to Rifugio Vittorio Emanuele II

GPS track: 4.8 km, 760 m up, 0 m down.

We reached Pont at about 5:20 and, after the rain, set off at about 7:30. The walk up took 2 hours 44 minutes. A quiet day, but the first effort after a long journey and the first real test of the rucksack.

10 June: Gran Paradiso

GPS track: 13.6 km, 1,270 m up, 2,030 m down.

We left at 4:50. Almost 1,300 m of climbing, a glacier, altitude and a demanding finish. We reached the rocks below the top at about 10:40 and the summit at about 11:20. It is a proper four-thousander and a good test. I saw that I was not the strongest in the group. It wasn’t pleasant, but I needed to see it.

Instead of a second night at Vittorio we went down to Pont and drove to France, which gave us more flexibility. More about this day in the acclimatisation guide.

11 June: Bellevue to Tête Rousse

GPS track: 5.9 km, 1,360 m up, 10 m down.

At about 10:00 we took the gondola from Les Houches to Bellevue and continued on foot: a traverse towards Mont Lachat, the Tramway du Mont-Blanc tracks, a tunnel, the Nid d’Aigle area, the first snow, rock and a long climb. The app did not always lead the obvious way, so we watched the terrain, the tracks and other people. We reached Tête Rousse at about 16:16. From the terrace you can already see the Grand Couloir and the Goûter high above.

12 June: Tête Rousse to the Goûter

GPS track: 1.4 km, 650 m up, 0 m down.

We started at about 7:01. Crossing the Grand Couloir takes little time, but it sits in your head long before. Then the long climb up the rocks of the Aiguille du Goûter. There are steel cables, but this is not a tourist via ferrata. It is high mountain terrain, with a rucksack, tired legs and exposure. We reached the Goûter at about 11:26.

13 June: summit day and back to the Goûter

GPS track: 8.1 km, 1,030 m up, 1,020 m down.

Breakfast at about 2:00, out at about 3:03, in the dark, with head torches and crampons. We were at the Vallot at about 5:38. It was cold and windy, and many teams were waiting or turning back. We waited too and only moved on at about 9:00. I don’t offer that as a model. That is simply how it went for us.

The Bosses ridge was hard on the mind: exposure, wind, crevasses and teams coming from both directions. We reached the summit in waves, from about 11:33 to 12:28, and were back at the Goûter at about 15:50. Only then could we relax a little.

14 June: the Goûter to Bellevue

GPS track: 7.5 km, 10 m up, 2,030 m down.

The descent is not a formality: the rocks of the Aiguille du Goûter, the Grand Couloir, Tête Rousse, the Refuge des Rognes area, the tracks and Bellevue. We heard stones in the couloir. A stone can fall by itself or be knocked down by a team above, so look up as well and stay out of the fall line. From Tête Rousse our rucksacks were heavier, because we picked up the things we had left there.
